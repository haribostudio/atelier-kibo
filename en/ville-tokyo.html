Tokyo

Tokyo is lived neighborhood by neighborhood, like cities sewn together. The steam of the yokocho under the rails, the silence of shrines at eight in the morning, the neon replacing daylight. We left with full notebooks — and these addresses, tested one by one, that we repeat to ourselves like mantras.

“Eight seats at the counter, in Golden Gai. The best dinner of the trip.” ✳︎
Shibuya Crossing, at nightphoto Benh Lieu Song — CC BY-SA ✳︎ while we wait for our own

Eat

Barubora-yaGolden Gai, Shinjuku

Eight seats, one counter, the chef cooking thirty centimeters away. Okonomiyaki, skewers, curry udon — the best dinner in Japan, no debate.

IshibashiSotokanda

Tokyo's oldest sukiyaki — 1870, older than the Eiffel Tower. Private tatami room, hostess in kimono, wagyu dipped in beaten egg. A ceremony. Book ahead.

Asakusa AmairoAsakusaInstagram

Family-run tempura with an airy batter, the master at the stove in front of you. Arrive at noon sharp — the line is part of the ritual.

Kayaba CoffeeYanakaInstagram

A restored 1938 kissaten, tatami upstairs. Breakfast from before the modern world, in the Tokyo of cats and cemeteries.

Sleep

The Edo SakuraIriyaInstagram

A small house set apart, between Ueno and Asakusa. Residential, silent Tokyo — the one not shown enough.

hotel MONday Akihabara-AsakusabashiAsakusabashiInstagram

The perfect base on the east side: Kuramae on foot, the Yamanote around the corner. Simple, clean, unbeatable.

Omoide Yokocho, Shinjukuphoto Dick Thomas Johnson — CC BY ✳︎ while we wait for our own

Party

Golden GaiShinjukuInstagram

Two hundred six-seat bars in six alleys. Start at Albatross — red velvet, chandeliers, Japanese whisky — and end wherever the night decides. Cash only.

Omoide YokochoWest Shinjuku

Lanterns and yakitori smoke under the rails. A drink at the counter of Nogataya, run by the old couple, before diving into Kabukichō.

Andaz Rooftop BarToranomon, 52nd floorInstagram

The most dizzying sunset in the city. Tokyo Tower on one side, Mount Fuji if the sky allows. Book, arrive before 7 pm.

Golden Gai, six alleys, two hundred barsphoto Alexkom000 — CC BY ✳︎ while we wait for our own

Shopping & beads

Oi Racecourse Flea MarketŌi, on Sundays

Tokyo's largest flea market — 500 stalls at the racecourse, 80 % locals. Kimonos, ceramics, cameras. Come early, bring cash in small bills.

KakimoriKuramaeInstagram

The made-to-order notebook: cover, papers, binding, engraving the same day. And all of Kuramae — Tokyo's Brooklyn — around it.

Re CameraTaitōInstagram

The cult camera shop. Ricoh GRs you can't find in Europe, vintage Contax, expert advice. You walk in curious, you walk out equipped.

ManiA — Furugi 83ShimokitazawaInstagram

Shimokita's signature vintage Disney, in the middle of the hundred-thrift-store district. Leave room in your suitcase.

Daikanyama T-SiteDaikanyamaInstagram

The most beautiful bookstore in the world, three pavilions set among the trees. You lose two hours there without meaning to.

Nezu Shrine, the torii tunnelphoto BWard 1997 — CC BY ✳︎ while we wait for our own

Encounters & rituals

Meiji-jingū at opening timeHarajuku

A forest in the middle of the city, giant cypress torii, the row of sake barrels. Be there at 8:30, before everyone else — the crunch of gravel, and nothing else.

Nezu ShrineNezuInstagram

The vermilion torii tunnel, the secret version of Fushimi Inari (1705). Azaleas in spring, silence all year round.

Zōjō-ji + Tokyo TowerShibakoen

The temple and the tower in the same frame — both Tokyos in one image. Go for the photo, stay for the calm.

teamLab PlanetsToyosuInstagram

Barefoot in the water, surrounded by lights. Book, go after nightfall, let yourself be swallowed.
