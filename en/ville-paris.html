Paris

Walk everywhere. Push every door. Cross the river twice.

Paris is a bad city to visit and a great one to live. You think you know it because you know its monuments — but the real Paris happens in hidden courtyards, nine-square-metre counters, haberdasheries where beads are weighed by hand, and cellars where records play after midnight. This is where our atelier is — where every KIBO piece is strung by hand. Here is our day — not the most famous one, ours. Walk between every address. That's where Paris happens.

BlondieInstagram For the first coffee.

Rue du Château d'Eau, a coffee shop that roasts its own beans and lets you know it quietly. Order the coffee of the day, sit by the window, watch the 10th arrondissement head to work. The day starts when the cup is empty — and the next stop is right across the street.

Cross the street: the bakery that made Paris babka-obsessed is at number 32, almost opposite Blondie. Grab one for the road — it won't survive until lunch, and that's exactly as it should be.

→ 32 rue du Château d'Eau, 10e

Fashion, art, fragrance — the edit that shakes up what “Parisian” means. You walk in for five minutes, you walk out with a different idea of your own wardrobe. The kind of place worth crossing town for.

A former artists' squat turned institution: six floors of studios open to the public, free, in the heart of Rivoli. Climb the painted staircase, push the doors, watch people work. It's exactly what an atelier should be — and we're well placed to say so.

Ralph's Lunch in the hidden courtyard.

Push through the carriage door at 173 boulevard Saint-Germain: behind the Ralph Lauren store hides a cobbled courtyard like nothing else in Paris. The burger is famous, the courtyard less so — and that's the real luxury.

→ 173 boulevard Saint-Germain, 6e
2:45 PM
Cravan Coffee by day, cocktails by night.

Two doors down from Ralph's, Franck Audoux's multi-storey house: precision coffee in the afternoon, cocktails at night. Take your coffee at the ground-floor counter, go up to see the salons — and keep the address, it changes faces after dark.

→ 165 boulevard Saint-Germain, 6e
3:33 PM
Jardin du Palais-Royal Lose track of time under the arcades.

Cross the Seine and head for the arcades: the Buren columns, green chairs in the sun, a Kitsuné coffee in hand. It's the pause that makes you lose track of time — in Paris, that's what gardens are for. And the next address is a five-minute walk away.

→ Place du Palais-Royal, 1er
5:21 PM
Ultramod Where necklaces begin.

A haberdashery untouched since 1832, rue de Choiseul: antique ribbons, trimmings, buttons by the thousand, wooden drawers that have never changed. Places like this are where necklaces begin — we speak from experience. Walk in without a plan, walk out with a treasure.

→ 3-4 rue de Choiseul, 2e

Rue Mouffetard, a rotisserie like almost none are left: the chickens turn, the jus drips onto the potatoes, and the sandwich that comes out beats every bistro around. Our absolute favorite — zero fuss, all real.

SUNDAY — OUTSIDE THE WALLS
Les Puces de Saint-Ouen — Marché Vernaison Sunday belongs to the flea market.

The world's largest antiques market begins where Paris ends. Go early, no list, cash in hand. These are the alleys where treasures turn up — antique jewelry, forgotten beads, objects with three lives behind them.

→ 99 rue des Rosiers, Saint-Ouen — Sat & Sun

The Paris Rules ✳︎

01Don't climb the Eiffel Tower.

Look at it from a rooftop, a bridge, a window. It's better from afar.

02Walk everywhere.

The métro steals the streets from you. This whole guide is done on foot — by design.

03Push every door.

Hidden courtyards are the real architecture of Paris. A carriage door left ajar is an invitation.

04Order the strange thing.

The wine you can't pronounce, the dish you can't translate.

05Cross the river twice a day.

Right bank in the morning, left bank at night — or the other way round. Paris changes personality when you cross a bridge.

06The quays after 10pm count as a monument.

A bottle, friends, feet dangling above the Seine. Free and unforgettable.

07Sunday belongs to the flea market.

The flea market, early, no list. See above.

08Leave one arrondissement unexplored.

You need a reason to come back.
