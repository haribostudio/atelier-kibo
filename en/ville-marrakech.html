Marrakech

Get lost. Stay curious. Eat with your hands.

Marrakech is not a city you visit in the right order. You can set out looking for a museum and end up in front of a 12-dirham sandwich, walk into a shop for five minutes and come out an hour later, step through a door that looks like nothing and discover a garden, an art collection, or someone offering you tea. It's here, between two counters of the medina, that the first KIBO necklaces were assembled — bead by bead. Our advice: keep a few addresses on your phone, but not too much of a plan. Walk. Look at the doors. And from time to time, put Google Maps away.

“The first necklace — we finished it that same evening, on the rooftop.” ✳︎

8:03 AM
Pronto Caffè For the first coffee.

Start before Marrakech is fully awake. On Riad Zitoun el Kdim, Pronto Caffè fits into almost nothing: a wooden counter, an espresso machine, a few square meters. Have a coffee — the spiced cortado is a good idea — then ask the barista where he'd go for lunch. People who make good coffee usually know other people who make good things.

→ Rue Riad Zitoun el Kdim, medina
9:17 AM
The Medina Take the long way.

Now, get lost. Not metaphorically: actually put your phone away for an hour. The streets of Marrakech have this strange ability to take you, in a few meters, from a crowded souk to a perfectly silent alley. If Google Maps says right and a street on the left looks more interesting — go left.

10:06 AM
Riad YimaInstagram The door you almost missed.

Somewhere behind Rahba Lakdima hides the riad-gallery of artist Hassan Hajjaj. Photography, furniture made from salvaged signs, saturated colors, Moroccan popular culture: his universe takes over the entire house. The entrance looks like that of a private home — go in, go up, have a tea. If you feel like you've got the wrong address, you're probably almost there.

→ 52 Derb Aarjane, Rahba Lakdima
11:02 AM
Mustapha BlaouiInstagram Marrakech's cabinet of curiosities.

Don't trust the facade. Behind 144 Arset Aouzal: stacked rugs, lanterns, tables, antiques, glassware, baskets. Less a shop than a place you dig through. Don't come with a list — ask to see what's hidden in the back.

→ 144 Arset Aouzal
The stalls of the medinaphoto Anja Disseldorp — CC BY ✳︎ while we wait for our own
12:41 PM
Chez Omar — Derb Dabachi Forget the tablecloth.

Time to stop with the beautiful addresses. Look for Chez Omar and the little snack stands around it: a hot plate, bread, harira, plastic stools. Order whatever goes fastest — the mixed sandwich is a good option. Don't check whether the place is in a guide. Look instead at who's eating next to you.

→ Derb Dabachi
1:16 PM
The Juice Guy The 10-dirham reset.

You'll pass dozens of them: a counter, hanging bananas, oranges, a blender that has seen a few million fruits go by. Orange if you want to keep it simple, avocado-banana if a drink can reasonably replace lunch. A few dirhams later, move on. No pin, no reservation, no Instagram. That's a city guide too.

2:04 PM
La FamilleInstagram Behind the door.

For a much slower lunch, save La Famille for another day. A discreet door on Derb Jdid hides a luminous garden and a mostly vegetarian kitchen, simple and seasonal. You walk in with the noise of the medina in your head and, five minutes later, everything has slowed down. Don't plan anything right after.

→ 34 Derb Jdid
3:12 PM
1112Instagram Twelve teas. One courtyard.

Near the Medersa Ben Youssef, push open the door: a riad, turquoise zellige, a patio, a house built around Moroccan tea. The menu explores regional traditions — mint, herbs, rose. Choose the one whose composition you understand the least. It's often a good method when traveling.

→ 12 Place de la Kissariat Ben Youssef
3:58 PM
Foundouk GargaaInstagram Disappear for an hour.

A foundouk was once a place where merchants, goods and animals stopped along the way. This one keeps something of that idea of refuge: a courtyard, a big tree, art, something to drink. Not a place to “do something” — a place to do nothing for an hour. Rare enough to earn its spot here.

→ 9 Rue de la Koutoubia
4:36 PM
La BoucherieInstagram Souvenirs for people who hate souvenirs.

Don't worry, it's not a butcher shop — that's exactly the point. A concept store that reworks Moroccan codes into T-shirts, illustrations and graphic objects. The place to bring back something from Marrakech that doesn't look like a Marrakech souvenir.

→ 126 Rue Riad Zitoun el Kdim
5:21 PM
Leave the medina Head to Sidi Ghanem.

Take a taxi. The transition is deliberately abrupt: you leave the pink walls and alleys for an industrial zone full of garages and warehouses. And that's precisely where part of contemporary creative Marrakech has settled.

5:42 PM
LRNCEInstagram Moroccan craft, rewritten.

Ceramics, textiles, painting, furniture, clothing — made in Marrakech with local artisans, but in a completely contemporary vocabulary. Not a nostalgic reproduction of Moroccan craft: a continuation. Look especially at the ceramics and textiles.

→ 59 Rue Sidi Ghanem
6:13 PM
JajjahInstagram Hassan Hajjaj's other Marrakech.

A few streets away, find Hassan Hajjaj again — Jajjah, his name almost reversed. Café, tea, restaurant, gallery and shop in an instantly recognizable Moroccan pop universe. Have a tea, look at the exhibition, and above all notice the contrast with the Marrakech of the morning. It's the same city.

→ 114–116 Rue Sidi Ghanem

Another afternoon

—
MACAALInstagram Africa, now.

In Al Maaden, the Al Maaden Museum of African Contemporary Art gathers painting, photography, sculpture and contemporary creation from Africa and its diaspora. The space is calm, far from the energy of the medina. Take your time, then stay for the café or the garden before heading back.

→ Al Maaden, Sidi Youssef Ben Ali

Night falls

6:57 PM
M Rooftop — Medina HeritageInstagram Art first. Sunset second.

Don't go straight to the roof: the route passes through an exhibition space and a concept store before reaching the rooftop. That's what makes the address interesting. Then order something and let Marrakech do the rest — at that hour, the walls change color every ten minutes.

→ 58 angle Rue El Ksour & Rue Fhal Zefriti
Jemaa el-Fna, the second seatingphoto Andrzej Kosiński — CC BY-SA ✳︎ while we wait for our own
7:26 PM
MÖ-MÖInstagram Watch Jemaa el-Fna switch on.

Climb to the terrace just before dark. Below, Jemaa el-Fna begins its second seating: the stalls appear, the lights come on, the smoke rises, the crowd changes. Don't come just for dinner. Come watch the square become Jemaa el-Fna.

→ 1 Rue Riad Zitoun el Kdim
The Koutoubia at golden hourphoto mattk1979 — CC BY-SA ✳︎ while we wait for our own
8:42 PM
Dar des ArtsInstagram One last sunset.

There are views you photograph, and others that make you put the phone down. From the terraces, Marrakech turns almost monochrome: earth, orange, dust, then blue. The secret isn't the sunset — it's the twenty minutes just before.

→ 50 Derb Tbib, Riad Zitoun el Jdid
10:37 PM
BaromètreInstagram Marrakech after dark.

End in Guéliz. Behind a discreet facade, a night laboratory: glassware, bottles, low light and cocktails built with far more precision than you'd expect from a last drink. Sit at the bar, watch them work, order something you would never have chosen. Then forget the time.

→ Rue Moulay Ali, Guéliz

Another morning

—
Thirty5iveInstagram Coffee. Vinyl. Slow down.

The next morning, Marrakech can wait. In Guéliz, a new generation of addresses: specialty coffee, minimalist lines, art, vinyl — and no desire to artificially recreate a medina indoors. Order a filter coffee, choose a record, stay.

→ 3 Rue El Imam Malik, Guéliz

Our favorite — beyond the walls

—
Kasbah d'IfAgafay Desert, 40 min awayInstagram Leave everything. Come back different.

When Marrakech gets too full, take the road to Amizmiz. A kasbah set between the Atlas and the Agafay desert — stone, earth, craft, silence. Go for a night or just for lunch facing the void, and come back to the medina as if washed clean. Our absolute favorite.

→ Route d'Amizmiz km 27, Tamesloht — Agafay Desert

The Marrakech Rules ✳︎

01Get lost once a day.

Without Google Maps. Even thirty minutes is enough.

02Eat somewhere with plastic chairs.

If your whole stay unfolds in beautiful riads, you'll have missed part of Marrakech.

03Ask who made it.

Before bargaining for an object, ask who made it and where. The conversation immediately becomes more interesting.

04Look behind doors.

In Marrakech, the facade rarely tells you what lies behind.

05Follow the locals at lunch.

Especially around Derb Dabachi. The small places called “Snack” are often a better bet than the most visible restaurants.

06Go to Sidi Ghanem.

Even if the industrial zone looks nothing like the Marrakech you had in mind.

07Look up.

Doors, ceilings, terraces, electric wires, minarets, painted signs. Marrakech happens as much above your head as in front of you.

08Never optimize Marrakech.

The best memory of the day will probably be something that wasn't planned.

One last thing

Marrakech can sometimes feel like it's been turned into a set. The perfect riads, the pools, the rooftops, the baskets, the terracotta walls. They exist, and some are magnificent. But the city becomes far more interesting when you start looking between the addresses. The juice seller. The guy fixing a scooter. The bread coming out of the communal oven. The café where no one speaks English. The door with no sign. The twelve-dirham sandwich after a contemporary art gallery. That's where the two Marrakechs begin to blend — and that's usually where the journey really begins.

« Don't try to see Marrakech. Let Marrakech happen to you. » ✳︎
